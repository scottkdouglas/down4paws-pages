Stop calling your dog for 48 hours

If your dog is somewhere between 6 months and 3 years old, "come" may have gotten worse, not better. They used to run to you. Now they look right at you and keep sniffing.

Your dog didn't forget their training. Their brain is growing up, and they're checking whether "come" still means come.

Most of us answer that question in ways that backfire, without meaning to. I see three habits all the time.

Three habits that backfire

The broken record. "Come. Come! COME!" Your dog learns the first call is only a suggestion.

Calling for things they hate. Bath time, nail trims, leaving the dog park. You'd start screening your calls too.

Chase or give up. When they don't come, we either chase them (fun game) or walk away (they win).

Your 48-hour reset

For the next 48 hours, stop calling your dog. Completely. Instead:

  • When you need them, walk over and gently guide them.
  • Hand them a treat when they wander over on their own.
  • Notice when they choose to be near you. That's useful information.

It will feel strange. That's okay. You're clearing out all the ignored calls so the word can mean something again. Worried about safety? That's what the leash is for.

What comes next

This reset comes from week one of Total Recall, my 30-day recall course by email. The rest of the course walks you through the 5 rules that make recall stick:

  1. Never call your dog for something unpleasant.
  2. Only call when you're sure they'll come.
  3. Make it happen, every time.
  4. Say it once.
  5. Pay your dog what they're worth.
